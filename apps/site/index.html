The work that used to teach judgment is being automated.

Early learning-by-doing work is shrinking

Diligence review, first drafts and issue lists were how associates built instincts. Those tasks now go to AI first.

Correct output is not understanding

AI can produce work, but inexperienced associates won't be able to explain it, and won't not know when it's wrong.

Tools to speed up work aren't built to teach judgment

Rushing to the finished product reduces deliberate practice, and the judgment that comes with it.

How it works

One deal, start to close, played out in an inbox.

1. Work the matter

Emails arrive from the partner, senior associate, client and lender's counsel. The first simulation is a borrower-side venture debt financing.

2. Explain your thinking

Colleagues ask how you see things, the way they would on a real deal. The reason matters as much as the call.

3. Get questioned, not corrected

The AI responds with questions about your reasoning. You stay the author of every answer.

4. Live with your choices

Early decisions come back later in the deal. It ends with a debrief to reinforce personalized learnings.

Early testers

We're looking for a handful of transactional associates to try it.

Who

Junior-to-mid associates in finance, venture, or emerging companies practices. Candor matters more than seniority.

What it takes

Work through one deal on your own schedule.

Safe for your firm

The deal is entirely synthetic. No client data, nothing touches firm systems.

Questions

Is it okay to do this while I'm at a firm?

The deal, the parties and the documents are all fictional. You never use client information or firm systems, and you can do it on your own device and your own time.

Who plays the partner, client and opposing counsel?

AI plays every other participant. The deal documents are reviewed by a practicing attorney so the matter reads like real work.

Who's building this?

A practicing finance associate and a software builder. We started re:matter because the routine work that used to train junior associates is disappearing, and nothing has replaced it yet.
